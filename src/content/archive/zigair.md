# ZigAir flight-sharing marketplace

ZigAir proposed a different way to access private charter travel: purchase a seat and share the flight's cost with other travelers. Its [company profile](https://www.linkedin.com/company/zigair) identifies it as a San Francisco travel marketplace founded in 2012 and describes that flight-sharing proposition.

The [AppealingStudio history](/archive/appealingstudio/) places ZigAir in the studio's 2012 project record. It adds an aviation marketplace to the broader history of web and product work that later included GetMyBoat.

## The product idea

Private charter is a substantial purchase when one customer carries the cost of the aircraft. ZigAir's proposition was to bring travelers together around shared flights, making the individual seat the unit of purchase. The company's profile summarized the idea as “Buy the Seat. Share the Plane. Lower the Price.” [ZigAir on LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/zigair).

[Sascha Mornell's historical biography](https://saschamornell.wordpress.com/about/) identifies Mornell and Raf Collado as co-founders and describes the company progressing from its initial idea to an alpha launch. That account supports the early company context; it does not establish booking volume or commercial results.

## The homepage experience

A historical homepage screenshot supplied for this project record shows a beta-branded interface built around a familiar travel search. Travelers could enter an origin and destination, choose departure and return dates, select a seat count, and start a search. A separate “List Plane” action gave aircraft providers a visible entry point.

The page paired the search form with a prominent aircraft photograph and a featured charter operator showing a location and per-seat price. Explanatory navigation offered visitors a way to understand the service before signing up. Together, those elements communicated both sides of the marketplace: a traveler looking for a seat and a provider offering aircraft capacity.

The screenshot records the interface and its marketing presentation at one point in time. Its advertised aircraft and airport counts are not treated here as verified inventory, and the displayed price is a historical example.

## Why this project belongs in the studio history

ZigAir illustrates a recurring product problem in marketplace work: translating a complex service into a search experience a customer can understand. In this interface, route, timing, and party size formed the starting point, while aircraft listing had its own path.

The studio record connects ZigAir to AppealingStudio's work. The available sources establish the product concept and company background, but do not specify my individual implementation responsibilities or the technology stack. This account therefore focuses on the documented product experience.
