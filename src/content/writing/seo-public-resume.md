# SEO for a public resume site.

A public resume site should do more than look good. It should make it obvious what the person does, what they have built, and why search should connect their name to those subjects.

[Back to writing index](/writing/) [Open resume](/resume/)

## Search target

Kiefer Waight plus the work itself: software engineer, full stack developer, React, Node.js, TypeScript, AWS, project history, and technical writing.

## What I want indexed

### Name and role

Kiefer Waight, software engineer, full-stack developer, systems-minded builder.

### Subject matter

React, Node.js, TypeScript, Next.js, AWS, Gatsby, Contentful, documentation.

### Proof of work

Projects, deep dives, resume pages, and public case studies that show how I work.

## The content structure matters

I do not want one giant page that tries to say everything. I want a content cluster: a homepage, a resume page, project pages, and writing pages that reinforce each other. That structure gives search engines more ways to understand what the site is about.

### Homepage

Introduces the name, role, and the overall public narrative.

### Resume

Provides a compact, search-friendly summary of experience and skills.

### Projects

Shows the concrete work behind the claims.

### Writing

Explains the thinking behind the work, which is often what people are actually searching for.

## The mechanics I rely on

- Clear page titles that start with the subject and the name.
- Canonical URLs for each page so the site does not split authority.
- Structured data that identifies the person and the content type.
- Internal links between the homepage, resume, projects, and essays.
- A sitemap so the page cluster is obvious to crawlers.
- Natural repeated phrasing for the terms I want associated with my name.

## What I avoid

### Keyword stuffing

Search engines are better at language now. Repetition without substance does not help.

### Single-page minimalism

A one-page portfolio makes it harder to create topic-specific landing pages.

### Hidden context

If the site does not explain the story behind the work, the site is leaving value on the table.
